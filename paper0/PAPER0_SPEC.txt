# Codex instructions: Paper 0

## Working title

**Transport-faithful probabilistic emulation and diagnostic assimilation of 3D tokamak-edge turbulence**

## Objective

Build and rigorously validate a probabilistic emulator of the existing TCV/Hermes turbulence simulations that:

1. Forecasts the joint evolution of the simulated plasma fields.
2. Preserves transport-relevant statistics on a completely held-out simulation.
3. Produces useful, calibrated forecast ensembles.
4. Assimilates sparse synthetic diagnostic observations using EnKF/ETKF/EnKS.
5. Ranks diagnostic configurations by their value for state and transport estimation.

The paper is primarily a plasma-physics/SciML paper. Do not force an unsupported core-ML novelty claim. The model comparison exists to identify a working stochastic representation and establish credibility.

The intended headline is:

> A transport-faithful probabilistic emulator of tokamak-edge turbulence enables fast state estimation and principled diagnostic ranking.

This claim must be treated as a hypothesis until the held-out evaluation supports it.

---

## Hard constraints

* Available data consist only of two TCV/Hermes runs: **85604 and 85606**.
* Use 85604 for training and validation.
* Keep 85606 completely blinded until model selection, metric definitions, assimilation settings, and acceptance criteria are frozen.
* Do not use Gkeyll, PIC, DIII-D, The Well, or other datasets in Paper 0.
* Do not use flux, spectra, cross-phase, conservation, PDE residuals, blob labels, or other physics-derived quantities as training losses.
* Physics quantities are evaluation metrics only.
* Do not claim device-wide, regime-wide, or broad cross-shot generalization from two runs.
* Do not treat temporal windows from one simulation as independent physical shots.
* Do not claim steering or control without action-conditioned simulations.
* Preserve all existing user changes in the repository.
* Use existing architecture and training infrastructure wherever possible.

---

## Paper 0 contribution

Paper 0 should answer three questions.

### Q1. What makes the current emulator fail?

Separate errors caused by:

* field compression;
* one-step dynamics;
* autoregressive rollout;
* stochastic representation;
* ensemble calibration;
* assimilation geometry.

### Q2. Can an existing or minimally repaired deep generative model preserve transport?

The decisive quantities include:

* density-potential cross-phase;
* cross-field coherence;
* directional spectra;
* particle and heat flux;
* flux distributions and tails;
* event-conditioned transport;
* ensemble calibration of fields and transport.

### Q3. Once validated, can the ensemble support diagnostic assimilation and diagnostic ranking?

Use the probabilistic emulator as the forecast prior in an ensemble filter. Rank synthetic diagnostic configurations according to how much they improve:

* hidden-field reconstruction;
* transport estimation;
* forecast horizon;
* posterior calibration.

The model sweep is supporting evidence. The paper is not titled or framed as a generic comparison of diffusion models.

# Phase 1: immutable data protocol

## Primary split

Use:

* **85604:** training and contiguous validation.
* **85606:** completely held-out final evaluation.

Within 85604:

1. Identify the statistically steady interval.
2. Construct one chronological training region.
3. Insert a guard interval longer than the maximum input-plus-target window.
4. Construct one chronological validation region after the guard interval.
5. Fit all normalization constants using the 85604 training region only.
6. Do not randomly shuffle frames before constructing the split.

Windows may be sampled randomly within the training region after leakage-safe boundaries have been established.

## Held-out test discipline

Before evaluating 85606, freeze:

* model architecture;
* checkpoint-selection rule;
* training budget;
* rollout horizon;
* ensemble size;
* diagnostic definitions;
* observation operators;
* observation noise;
* filter inflation and localization;
* diagnostic-ranking criteria;
* plotting conventions.

After opening 85606 results, do not tune based on them. Bug fixes must be documented, and any affected evaluation must be rerun consistently for all models.

## Canonical manifest

Create a machine-readable manifest containing:

* run ID;
* simulator;
* device and geometry;
* grid and coordinates;
* field names and units;
* cadence and time units;
* simulated toroidal fraction;
* boundary conditions;
* forcing and heating metadata if available;
* burn-in and steady-state intervals;
* split designation;
* blind-test flag;
* known data-quality issues.

Verify the stored cadence, currently believed to be approximately 3.1319 microseconds.

Verify whether the simulated toroidal domain represents approximately one fifth of the torus and whether Fourier index (k) maps to toroidal mode number (n=5k). Never assume this mapping silently.

# Phase 2: metric validation and oracle ladder

## Decorrelation time

Estimate a representative decorrelation time using only the 85604 training region and a frozen procedure.

Report forecast horizons in:

* frames;
* microseconds;
* estimated decorrelation times.

Before decorrelation, emphasize trajectory accuracy and calibration. After decorrelation, emphasize distributions, spectra, transport, and invariant statistical behavior.

## Oracle ladder

Implement and evaluate:

* **O0: Diagnostic oracle.** Validate every metric on synthetic fields with known answers.
* **O1: Codec reconstruction.** Ground truth through encoder and decoder.
* **O2: Teacher-forced one-step forecast.**
* **O3: Fixed (K)-step forecast block.**
* **O4: Autonomous free rollout.**
* **O5: Periodic state or latent reset using encoded ground truth.**

This ladder determines where transport information is lost.

If the codec does not reconstruct cross-phase and flux adequately, stop dynamics development and repair or replace the representation first.

## Required metric tests

Add automated tests for:

* normalization round trips;
* axis and time ordering;
* split leakage;
* known Fourier modes;
* toroidal mode-number mapping;
* known cross-phase;
* known coherence;
* trusted flux calculation;
* geometry-region masks;
* ensemble tensor shape;
* member-wise nonlinear diagnostics;
* fair CRPS;
* no use of future truth during forecasting;
* no use of 85606 in model selection.

## B1: current deterministic ViT3D

## B2: current LOLA/diffusion model

Measure:

* diversity;

## B3: FGN-style functional generative model

Port the functional-generative mechanism into the strongest deterministic backbone.

Do not reproduce the entire weather-specific architecture. Introduce low-dimensional global noise through parameter-shared conditional normalization such as AdaLN, conditional LayerNorm, or FiLM.

Primary configuration:

* sample global (z_t);
* use the same (z_t) across spatial positions and network layers through shared conditioning;
* resample aleatoric noise at each autoregressive step;
* train using fair CRPS on marginal forecasts;
* use two ensemble samples per training example if memory requires;
* start with one model seed;
* use three independently trained model seeds for final results if computationally feasible.

Required FGN ablations:

* noise disabled;
* global functional noise;
* matched input noise;
* matched output noise;
* spatially independent noise;
* per-step versus trajectory-constant noise;
* one model versus deep ensemble where feasible.

Report:

* within-model aleatoric spread;
* between-model spread;
* combined spread;
* single-pass inference cost.

## B4: PDE-Refiner

Implement or port PDE-Refiner using a comparable backbone and compute budget.

Determine whether iterative refinement repairs the loss of non-dominant spectral modes and improves free rollout.

## B5: deterministic mean plus joint stochastic residual

Use:

[
x_{\mathrm{future}}
===================

\mu_\theta(\mathrm{context})
+
r_\phi(\mathrm{context},\mu_\theta,\epsilon).
]

Procedure:

1. Train or reuse the strongest deterministic mean predictor.
2. Freeze it initially.
3. Construct residual targets jointly across all fields and future times.
4. Train a conditional diffusion or flow model on the joint residual.
5. Fine-tune end to end only after the frozen-residual experiment works.

Prefer field-space residual generation. Use latent-space residual generation only if the residual codec passes the oracle tests.

Independent per-field residual generators are an ablation, not the primary method.

# Phase 4: select the forecast model

Use 85604 validation only.

Select the simplest model that satisfies the forecast requirements. Do not select exclusively by RMSE.

Evaluate:

## Field metrics

* RMSE;
* MAE;
* bias;
* variance;
* anomaly correlation;
* error versus horizon.

## Spectral metrics

* directional spectra;
* spectral slopes;
* coherent-band power;
* mode lifetimes;
* high-mode decay;
* the known useful band around (n\approx20) to (35).

## Cross-field metrics

For fields (a) and (b), calculate:

[
S_{ab}(k)=\langle a_k b_k^*\rangle,
]

[
\gamma_{ab}^2(k)
================

\frac{|S_{ab}(k)|^2}{S_{aa}(k)S_{bb}(k)},
]

[
\phi_{ab}(k)=\arg S_{ab}(k).
]

## Transport metrics

Use the authoritative geometry-aware particle and heat-flux implementations.

Report:

* mean radial profiles;
* sign;
* temporal distributions;
* probability tails;
* regional contributions;
* event-conditioned transport;
* error versus forecast horizon.

Compute nonlinear quantities separately for every ensemble member. Never compute flux only from the ensemble-mean fields.

## Calibration metrics

Report:

* CRPS;
* spread-skill relationship;
* empirical coverage;
* PIT or rank histograms;
* calibration of important modes;
* calibration of particle and heat flux.

## Spatial regions

Use authoritative masks for:

* confined edge;
* outboard midplane;
* separatrix;
* scrape-off layer;
* X-point neighborhood;
* divertor legs.

Do not infer these regions from image coordinates alone.

# Forecast acceptance gate

Do not proceed to diagnostic-ranking claims until the selected emulator:

1. Passes codec reconstruction checks.
2. Beats persistence and the deterministic baseline over meaningful short horizons.
3. Preserves the relevant spectral band materially better than the current failed rollout.
4. Preserves cross-phase and coherence sufficiently to reproduce the sign and approximate magnitude/distribution of transport.
5. Produces non-collapsed forecast ensembles.
6. Is not catastrophically miscalibrated.
7. Shows the same qualitative conclusions on held-out 85606 without tuning.

If no model passes, report the failure honestly and do not use its ensemble covariance to make strong diagnostic-design claims.

# Phase 5: synthetic diagnostic assimilation

## Observation model

Define:

[
y_t = H(x_t) + \nu_t,
\qquad
\nu_t\sim\mathcal N(0,R).
]

Implement observation operators only for diagnostics that can be defined credibly from the simulated fields.

Possible diagnostic families include:

* point or localized measurements;
* field-specific measurements of density, potential, or temperature;
* line-integrated or chord-like measurements;
* camera-like spatial projections;
* combinations of diagnostic families.

Clearly label every diagnostic as synthetic. Do not claim direct experimental realism unless Ben confirms the observation operator, cadence, resolution, and noise model.

## Filter baselines

Compare:

* no assimilation;
* persistence with assimilation where meaningful;
* latent ETKF/EnKF;
* field-space EnKF if computationally feasible;
* EnKS for offline smoothing;
* periodic oracle resets as an upper bound.

Use the generative emulator ensemble as the forecast prior.

Tune only on 85604 validation:

* inflation;
* localization;
* observation-noise assumptions;
* update cadence;
* ensemble size;
* latent versus field update choices.

Freeze all settings before 85606.

## Assimilation metrics

Measure:

* posterior field RMSE;
* posterior transport error;
* forecast error after each update;
* time until forecast divergence;
* posterior spread and coverage;
* filter stability;
* effective ensemble rank;
* update magnitude by latent or field direction;
* recovery of unobserved fields from observed fields.

Test whether a diagnostic can improve the target transport quantity even when it does not directly observe every field entering that quantity.

# Phase 6: diagnostic ranking

This is a central Paper 0 deliverable.

## Fair comparison

Compare diagnostic configurations under matched budgets:

* same number of measurements;
* same update cadence;
* comparable noise assumptions;
* comparable spatial coverage or an explicitly stated cost model.

Evaluate multiple sensor budgets rather than only one arbitrary configuration.

## Ranking criteria

Rank diagnostics separately by:

1. Full-state posterior accuracy.
2. Particle-flux posterior accuracy.
3. Heat-flux posterior accuracy.
4. Extension of useful forecast horizon.
5. Posterior calibration.
6. Robustness across temporal blocks and model seeds.
7. Information gained per measurement or per assumed diagnostic cost.

Do not collapse everything into one undocumented scalar score.

Produce:

* rankings for each objective;
* a Pareto frontier across state accuracy, transport accuracy, and measurement cost;
* uncertainty intervals on rankings;
* sensitivity to assumed noise and cadence.

An important possible result is that the diagnostic best for pixelwise state reconstruction is not the diagnostic best for transport inference.

Because there is only one held-out run, describe the final ranking as a held-out TCV/Hermes case study, not a universal experimental-design result.

# Statistical protocol

The physical unit of independence is the simulation run, not the temporal window.

With one training run and one held-out run:

* use block bootstrap intervals within 85606;
* choose block lengths longer than the measured autocorrelation time;
* report intervals conditional on the held-out run;
* include at least three model-training seeds when computationally feasible;
* distinguish temporal sampling uncertainty from model-seed uncertainty;
* do not report window counts as independent sample sizes.

A reverse-shot experiment may be performed only after the primary 85604-to-85606 result is frozen. Treat it as a sensitivity analysis, not an independent confirmation.

# Claims and scope

## Physics claim

If supported:

> A data-only probabilistic emulator trained on one 3D TCV/Hermes simulation preserves transport-relevant joint statistics on a second held-out simulation.

## Assimilation claim

If supported:

> The emulator’s forecast ensemble can serve as an effective transition prior for sparse diagnostic assimilation, improving estimation of both fields and turbulent transport.

## Diagnostic-design claim

If supported:

> Synthetic diagnostic families differ substantially in their ability to constrain hidden fields and transport, and ranking them by downstream transport information produces different conclusions than ranking them by field reconstruction alone.

## Limited ML insight

If supported:

> Sharp forecasts, low field error, and marginal calibration do not guarantee preservation of the joint cross-field distribution required for nonlinear scientific functionals and ensemble assimilation.

Do not claim a generally new probabilistic forecasting architecture unless a genuinely new method is implemented and validated beyond this dataset.

## Explicit limitations

State clearly:

* only two TCV/Hermes runs;
* one train/validation run and one held-out run;
* simulation-to-simulation evaluation;
* synthetic diagnostics;
* no experimental TCV measurements;
* no demonstrated causal steering;
* no broad device or operating-regime generalization.

# Decision rules

* If the codec fails, repair representation before dynamics.
* If PDE-Refiner solves the spectral and transport failure, use it.
* If FGN works, prefer it over more expensive diffusion unless diffusion provides a material benefit.
* If FGN is calibrated marginally but transport remains wrong, conclude that marginal CRPS is insufficient here.
* If joint residual generation beats independent residual generation, emphasize cross-field jointness.
* If all forecast models fail, stop before strong diagnostic-ranking claims and write the result as a benchmark/failure analysis.
* If forecast ensembles pass but EnKF fails, investigate ensemble covariance, observation geometry, localization, and latent observability.
* If diagnostic rankings are unstable across time blocks or noise assumptions, report the instability instead of presenting a definitive ranking.
* Do not begin Gkeyll/PIC pretraining, novel object-token architectures, or RL inside Paper 0.

The end state of Paper 0 is not “we ran ablations.” It is:

> a validated transport-faithful emulator, an honest held-out evaluation, a working assimilation system, and a defensible ranking of which synthetic diagnostics best constrain the plasma quantities we care about.
